A Ceremony Added Two Weeks Out at Boulder Flower Farm

Jono and Sloane booked me for their reception. Two weeks before the wedding, they asked me to run the ceremony too, at an outdoor spot with no power nearby, plus a live band and a hard 10 PM stop. Here's how it came together.

Couple
Jono and Sloane Krawczyk

Coordination

Ceremony sound, dinner, DJ sets, announcements

The event

Boulder Flower Farm is exactly what it sounds like: rows of flowers, big trees and open sky just outside Longmont. Jono and Sloane planned their ceremony under draped trees, then dinner and dancing under a big reception tent. Groove 'N Motion was their live band, and Urban Events ran the day.

The original plan for me was the reception. I'd play the dinner set, hand off to the band for their live set, then take over the dance floor through the end of the night.

What changed

About two weeks before the wedding, the plan grew. Jono and Sloane wanted me to run the ceremony sound too.

That's a whole second setup. The ceremony spot was outdoors, away from the tent, with no outlet nearby. Guests needed to hear every word of the vows. The officiant needed a hands-free mic, and the couple needed a mic for their vows. And the ceremony music had to start and stop on cue.

What I did

We updated the contract, and I planned the ceremony like its own event.

  • Power: I ran the ceremony on battery power, so the sound could go right where the couple wanted it, with no cords running across the lawn.
  • Sound: Two powered speakers on stands, linked together and aimed at the seating so the back rows heard as clearly as the front.
  • Mics: A wireless handheld for the vows and a clip-on lavalier for the officiant.
  • Music: Ceremony songs downloaded ahead of time and cued live as the wedding party walked.

Then the reception. I played the dinner set from the playlists Jono and Sloane sent me, reading the room and mixing in requests. When it was time for the band, I swapped over so Groove 'N Motion could play their set. When they finished, I took the dance floor through the band's own system, so there was no second wall of speakers and no gap in the energy. Two dance floor lights in the tent did the rest after dark.

The venue has a hard 10 PM stop, so we built the night backward from it. The last song and the closing announcement landed right on time.

Planning a wedding at Boulder Flower Farm?

  • Plan power for the ceremony site early. Battery-powered sound keeps the ceremony wherever you want it.
  • If you have a band, ask your DJ to run through the band's system. It keeps the tent clean and the transitions smooth.
  • Build your timeline backward from the venue's end time so the last dance isn't rushed.
  • A good day-of coordinator makes the handoffs easier. Urban Events ran the day at Jono and Sloane's wedding.
