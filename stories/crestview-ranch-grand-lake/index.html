A Windstorm Four Hours Before the Wedding at Crestview Ranch

Michael and Tawny rented Crestview Ranch for the whole weekend. The night before was perfect. Then, at noon on the wedding day, a windstorm rolled in, and we found out the ceremony site had no power. Here's how it still turned into the party of their lives.

Couple
Michael and Tawny Lindeman
Venue
Crestview Ranch, near Grand Lake
Date
June 20, 2026
Weather
Beautiful morning, windstorm by noon
I handled
Ceremony sound, cocktail hour, dinner, first dances, reception, after-party

The event

Michael and Tawny's plan was a full weekend in the mountains. They rented the whole ranch, and the wedding party stayed on site from Friday through Sunday.

I drove up Friday for the walkthrough. We timed out the ceremony, practiced the music cues and walked through every part of the setup. It was a gorgeous mountain evening.

That night, they asked if I'd run some music for the wedding party, just for fun. It wasn't part of the plan. I pulled out the speakers I'd brought, we plugged in everyone's phones, and the kitchen turned into a dance party. They gave me a room for the weekend, and from then on I was part of the crew.

What changed

The ceremony was at 4:00 on Saturday. I started setting up at 11. When my gear is ready and there's still work to do, I help, so I was laying tablecloths and helping build the cocktail hour and dinner areas outside.

At noon, a windstorm came through. Everything we'd set up outside had to come down. Glassware went inside, tablecloths came off, and it was clear the wind wasn't going anywhere that afternoon. Cocktail hour and dinner had to move indoors.

Then we found out the ceremony site, a long way from the house, had no power. No outlets, no generator, no battery packs. Nobody had mentioned it until that morning.

What I did

  • Power for the ceremony: I drove into town and bought two 100-foot extension cords, then ran power all the way out to the ceremony site.
  • Wind-proofing: I grabbed clamps for the tablecloths while I was in town, and set up two speakers and a microphone at the ceremony site built to hold up in the gusts.
  • A new floor plan: Cocktail hour moved into the living room. Dinner moved to a separate building. I built the DJ booth in the living room for the first dances, the reception and the after-party.
  • Leapfrogging speakers: Nobody had planned for sound in three separate spaces. With more notice, I would have rented extra speakers. Instead, I set one speaker up for cocktail hour and one for dinner, then carried the ceremony speakers from space to space as the night moved: ceremony, cocktail hour, dinner, then back to the booth. Every room had music right when it needed it.
  • Everything else: wherever an extra set of hands helped, I jumped in, without stepping on anyone's toes.

None of that was in my contract. It didn't matter. When you hire me, you get someone who has worked a lot of events and knows how to keep one moving, not just someone who presses play.

The result

The ceremony was windy from start to finish, and every word still came through. Dinner went perfectly. Then we moved the speakers back to the booth, did the first dances and opened the floor, and you could feel the tension leave the room.

It turned into the party of their lives. I played late into the night, stayed over and packed up the next morning. Guests told Michael and Tawny I was the best wedding DJ they'd ever seen, and I started booking guests' weddings that same night.

Planning a ranch or mountain weekend wedding?

  • Ask where the power is at every spot you plan to use: ceremony, cocktail hour, dinner and dancing.
  • Have a wind and weather plan for each space, not just the ceremony. Mountain afternoons change fast.
  • Tell your DJ about every space that needs music, so the right number of speakers shows up.
  • Hire vendors who will jump in when the plan changes. It happens at almost every outdoor wedding.
