How the checker decides

It thinks in areas, not just headcount. A hundred guests in a compact ballroom is a very different job from a hundred guests spread across a lawn, so it sizes each part of the day on its own: the ceremony, cocktail hour, dinner and the dance floor.

Typical dance floor starting points, indoors:

GuestsDance floor
Up to 752 speakers, subwoofer optional
76 to 1502 speakers and 1 subwoofer
151 to 2502 speakers and 2 subwoofers, or 4 speakers in a long room
More than 2504 speakers and 2 subwoofers
  • Outdoors with no tent: plan one step up. There are no walls to bounce the sound back.
  • Ceremony: a pair of speakers aimed at the seats, a clip-on mic for the officiant and a handheld for vows and readings. Big outdoor crowds get fill speakers partway back.
  • No power: battery-powered speakers and wireless mics, so the ceremony can happen anywhere.
  • With a band: one shared system. The DJ plugs into the band's mixer for DJ sets.

Questions

Is this the exact gear we'll get?

It's the plan I'd start from. Every venue is different, so I confirm the setup after a quick call about your space, or a walkthrough when it helps.

Do we really need a subwoofer?

For dancing, usually yes. Dance music lives in the low end and a floor without it feels thin. For dinner and the ceremony, no. In a small room it's optional.

What if the ceremony spot has no power?

Battery-powered speakers and wireless mics handle it. If a generator is the only option, place it as far from guests as you can, because generators are loud.

We have a band. Do we need DJ speakers too?

Usually only for the ceremony, cocktail hour and dinner, before the band is set up. For DJ sets, the DJ plugs into the band's mixer, so one system covers the dance floor.

Can you use the venue's sound system?

Often, yes, for speeches and background music. House systems are rarely built for a full dance floor, so I check what's there and bring only what's missing.
