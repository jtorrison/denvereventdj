Tips for a timeline that actually runs on time

  • Work backward from the end time. Many venues have a hard music curfew. Your last dance should land a few minutes before it, not get cut off.
  • Protect the dance floor. Two hours or more of open dancing is a good target. Long dinners and long cocktail hours eat into it fast.
  • Do the first dance early if you want the floor to open on your terms later, or right after dinner if you want it to kick off the party.
  • Put toasts during dinner. Guests are seated and fed, and nobody's waiting.
  • Share it with every vendor. Your DJ, photographer, planner and caterer should all work off the same version.
  • Leave room for weather. Outdoor and mountain weddings need a backup plan and a little slack. I learned that the hard way at Crestview Ranch.

Questions

How long is a typical wedding reception?

About four to five hours from the grand entrance to the last song is common. Add the ceremony and cocktail hour and most weddings need five to seven hours of music in total.

When should toasts happen?

During dinner, once everyone has food. Guests are seated, nobody is waiting, and the toasts don't cut into dancing.

When should we do the first dance?

Either right after the grand entrance, while everyone is already watching, or right after dinner to open the dance floor. Both work. Pick the one that fits the energy you want.

How much time should we leave for dancing?

Aim for at least two hours of open dancing. That's usually the part of the night guests remember most.

Is this timeline a plan I can actually use?

It's a strong starting point. Every wedding has its own details, like photos, traditions, travel between spaces and venue rules, so walk through it with your planner and DJ before the day.
