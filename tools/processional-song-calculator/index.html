How the math works

Here's the same calculation by hand, so you can check it even without the calculator.

  1. Time to walk the aisle. At a natural wedding pace, people cover about 2 feet per second. A 50-foot aisle takes about 25 seconds. Slow and formal is closer to 1.5 feet per second.
  2. Count the walks. Ten people walking alone is 10 walks. Ten people walking in pairs is 5 walks.
  3. Add the spacing. Each walker usually starts when the person ahead is about halfway down the aisle, roughly 10 seconds later.
  4. Add a little buffer. About 10 seconds for the last person to reach their spot before the music fades.

So the wedding party needs: (walks minus 1) times the spacing, plus one walk down the aisle, plus 10 seconds.

The entrance for the bride or couple gets its own song. Plan on a pause at the top of the aisle while everyone stands, a slower walk, and a moment at the front for hugs and the handoff. For a 50-foot aisle, that's usually about a minute.

Questions

How long should a processional song be?

Long enough to cover every walker plus a few seconds of buffer. For most weddings that's between 1 and 4 minutes for the wedding party, and about a minute for the couple's entrance. The calculator above works it out from your aisle length and how many people are walking.

How far apart should people walk down the aisle?

The classic rule is to start the next person when the one ahead is about halfway down the aisle. That's roughly 10 seconds apart at a natural pace. Closer than that feels rushed. Much farther feels slow.

What if my favorite song is too short for the processional?

Keep the song. A DJ can make a custom edit that repeats the best section so it lasts the whole walk, without anyone noticing the seam.

Should the bride or couple have a separate song?

Most couples do. Switching songs signals to everyone that it's time to stand, and it gives the entrance its own moment.

Who starts and stops the music?

Ideally your DJ, watching the aisle. A playlist can't see when the last bridesmaid reaches the front, which is why live cueing matters for the ceremony.
