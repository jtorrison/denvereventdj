Outdoor & Backyard Weddings

Farms, tents and backyards, with sound that works out there.

No walls, no outlets, no problem.

Outdoor weddings are beautiful and a little unpredictable. Sound disappears without walls, the nearest outlet is a long cord away, and the neighbors are part of the plan. Here's how I handle it.

What outdoor weddings need

Power you can count on

For ceremonies away from an outlet, I run battery-powered speakers and wireless mics. At Boulder Flower Farm, the whole ceremony ran on battery power under the trees.

Enough sound for open air

Outdoors, there's nothing for sound to bounce off, so it fades faster than you'd expect. Speakers need to be placed and aimed so the back rows hear the vows as clearly as the front.

Mics that beat the wind

A clip-on mic for the officiant and a handheld for vows, placed with the wind in mind.

Tents

Tents are great for dancing, but they're long and the sound changes from one end to the other. I set up so the dance floor hits and the tables can still talk.

Neighbors and curfews

Backyards and farm venues often have hard stops. We'll plan the timeline backward from it so the last dance happens on time and nobody's party gets cut off mid-song.

Backyard weddings feel like family.

Some of my favorite nights are in backyards, including the Romero wedding in Lakewood, which happened to be my brother's. The setup is simpler, but the details matter more: where the speakers go, how loud is too loud for the block, and keeping a dance floor going on the lawn.

Outdoor wedding questions

Can you play a backyard wedding without bothering the neighbors?

Yes. I size and aim the sound for your space, and we'll plan around your city's noise rules so the party ends on your terms, not with a knock on the door.

What if it rains?

We'll plan the backup before the day: the tent, the garage or the indoor space, where the power is there, and how fast we can move. The sound plan works in both places.

Do you bring lighting for after dark?

Yes. Dance floor lighting is included, and it makes a big difference in a tent or backyard once the sun goes down.

Our ceremony and reception are in different spots. Is that a problem?

Not at all. I'll either set up two systems or build in time to move gear, and I'll coordinate it with your planner so guests never notice.
