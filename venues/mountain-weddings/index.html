What's different about a mountain wedding

Getting there

Mountain venues can mean long drives, a pass, a dirt road and a long carry from parking. I build in extra time so I'm set up and sound-checked well before guests arrive, no matter what I-70 is doing.

Power

The best ceremony spot is rarely next to an outlet. I bring battery-powered speakers and wireless mics so you can say your vows right where the view is.

Weather

Summer afternoons in the Colorado mountains can bring a storm out of nowhere, and evenings get cold. We'll know the backup plan before the day, and the sound plan will work there too.

Wind

Wind is the enemy of microphones. A clip-on mic for the officiant, placed out of the wind, keeps every word clear.

No cell service

Every song I need is downloaded before I leave home. Your music never depends on a signal.

Quiet hours

Sound carries in open valleys, and many mountain venues and towns have firm music curfews. We'll plan the timeline so the last song lands right on time.

Mountain wedding questions

Do you travel to Vail, Breckenridge, Estes Park and other mountain towns?

Yes. I DJ weddings all over Colorado, from Grand Lake to Ruedi Reservoir to Aspen. Travel details go in your quote up front, so there are no surprises.

Can you do a ceremony at an overlook with no power?

Yes. I bring battery-powered speakers and wireless mics, so the ceremony can happen right where the view is.

What happens if weather moves us inside?

We plan for it ahead of time. I'll know the backup space, where the power is there, and how long the move takes, so the sound is ready wherever you end up.

Do you need anything from us for a remote venue?

Just the details: the load-in route, how far it is from parking to the ceremony and reception, the venue's quiet hours, and whether there's cell service. I handle the rest.

Getting married in the mountains?

Send me the venue and the date. I'll tell you what the site needs and how we'll handle power, weather and timing.
