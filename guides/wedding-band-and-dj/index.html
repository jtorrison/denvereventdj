Having a Band and a DJ at Your Wedding

A live band brings energy nothing else can. A DJ covers everything around it: the ceremony, cocktail hour, dinner, the band's breaks and the late-night dance floor. Here's how to plan both so the night feels like one party, not two acts.

By Josh Torrison, Denver wedding and event DJ since 2011

I love playing weddings with a band. Done right, guests don't notice where the band stops and the DJ starts. The night just keeps going. Done wrong, you get dead air, two different volume levels and a dance floor that empties every time the music changes hands.

Why couples book both

A band is a show. A DJ is a safety net and a specialist. Together they cover things neither does well alone:

  • The original recordings. Your first dance, parent dances and entrance songs, exactly as you know them.
  • The parts of the day before the band plays. Ceremony music, cocktail hour and dinner, often in different spaces.
  • The band's breaks. No silence, no stalled dance floor.
  • Requests and range. A band has a set list. A DJ can play anything, from the song Grandma asked for to the late-night hit your friends will lose it over.
  • The end of the night. Many bands play a fixed number of sets. A DJ can take it to the last song.

Who plays when

Every band works a little differently, so confirm their set lengths and breaks. Here's a split that works for a lot of weddings:

Part of the nightWho's on
CeremonyDJ (or ceremony musicians, with the DJ on mics and sound)
Cocktail hourDJ
Grand entrance and dinnerDJ
ToastsDJ on the mics
First danceBand live, or the DJ plays the recording
Open dancingBand sets, with the DJ covering breaks
Late night to last danceDJ

One sound system, not two

The cleanest setup is one set of speakers. The band brings its PA and mixer, and the DJ plugs into the band's mixer for DJ sets. That means one volume level, one sound and no second wall of speakers in your photos.

The DJ still brings a small system for the ceremony, cocktail hour and dinner, which usually happen before the band is set up or in another space. There's a diagram of the shared setup in how many speakers you need.

Smooth handoffs

The rule is simple: when one stops, the other is already playing. A few things make that happen:

  • Agree on the handoff songs. The band's last song and the DJ's first song should match in energy. Coming off a big band closer, I start with something people already know and keep the floor moving.
  • One voice on the mic. Pick the bandleader or the DJ to run announcements, and stick with it.
  • A shared timeline. Everyone works from the same run of show, with set times and the venue's end time.
  • Talk before the day. A quick call or a few texts with the bandleader about gear, power and set times saves a lot of scrambling.

What to settle ahead of time

  • Mics. Who provides the mics for the ceremony and toasts?
  • Power. Bands draw a lot of power. Ask the venue for enough outlets on separate circuits, especially in a tent.
  • Placement. Where the stage and the DJ booth go, so they share the system and both face the dance floor.
  • Load-in. Who sets up first, and when.
  • Your lists. Send the same must-play and do-not-play lists to both. The wedding music planner puts it on one page.
  • The end time. Who plays the last song, and how the night closes.

A real example: Boulder Flower Farm

At Jono and Sloane's wedding at Boulder Flower Farm, Groove 'N Motion was the band and Urban Events ran the day. I ran the ceremony on battery power, played dinner, then swapped over so the band could play their set. When they finished, I took the dance floor through the band's own system until the venue's 10 PM stop. No second set of speakers, no gap in the energy, and the last song landed right on time.

My take

Book the band for the show and the DJ for everything around it. Then get them talking before the wedding.

Quick answers

Do we need a DJ if we have a band?

Usually, yes, for part of the day. Bands rarely play the ceremony, cocktail hour and dinner, and they take breaks. A DJ covers all of that, plays the exact recordings you want for big moments and keeps the dance floor going after the band's last set.

Can the band and the DJ share speakers?

Yes, and it's usually the best setup. The DJ plugs into the band's mixer for DJ sets, so there's one set of speakers, one volume level and no gap during handoffs.

Who should MC the night?

Pick one voice. It can be the bandleader or the DJ, but one person should run the announcements so the night feels connected. Most bands are happy to let the DJ handle it when the DJ is there all day.

What does the DJ do while the band plays?

Gets the next handoff ready, keeps an eye on the timeline and checks in with the coordinator. When the band's set ends, music should already be playing.
