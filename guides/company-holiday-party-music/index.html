Company Holiday Party Music: A Planner's Guide

A great company holiday party has a shape: easy music while people arrive, a short program, then a real celebration. Here's how to plan the music for each part, keep it right for the room and actually get coworkers dancing.

By Josh Torrison, Denver wedding and event DJ since 2011

In this guide

  2. Arrival and networking
  3. Speeches, awards and the program
  4. Getting coworkers to dance
  5. Keep it right for the room
  6. Logistics planners forget

Holiday parties are some of my favorite events, because the room starts as coworkers and ends as a party. The music does a lot of that work, but only if it has a plan.

Plan the shape of the night

Most company parties follow the same arc:

Part of the nightWhat the music does
Arrival and networkingEasy, upbeat, low enough to talk over
Dinner or heavy appsWarm background, still conversational
Program: welcome, speeches, awardsWalk-up music, clean transitions, clear mics
CelebrationThe energy goes up and the dance floor opens
Last callA big singalong to close, then lights up on time

Send your DJ a run of show with times, who's speaking and in what order. That's what keeps the night tight.

Arrival and networking

The first hour sets the mood. People are walking in from work, finding their teams and grabbing a drink. The music should feel like a party without competing with conversation. A few holiday classics fit here. Wall-to-wall holiday music doesn't. Not everyone celebrates the same holidays, and by the third version of the same song, the room is over it.

Speeches, awards and the program

  • Wireless mics for every speaker, plus a spare for awards or a Q&A.
  • Walk-up music for each award winner. It's a small touch that makes people feel celebrated.
  • A tight program. Shorter is better. The longer it runs, the harder it is to get the energy back.
  • Sound that reaches the back. In a long room, fill speakers keep the back tables in the program. See the setup diagram on the corporate events page.

Getting coworkers to dance

  • Open the floor right after the program. The room is already gathered and paying attention. Go straight into something everyone knows.
  • Leadership on the floor. When the CEO dances, everyone else feels allowed to.
  • Play for every generation. Your team spans decades. A DJ who reads the room moves between eras so everyone gets a turn.
  • Lights change the room. Dimming the house lights and adding dance floor lighting turns a ballroom or office into a night out. More on lighting on the parties page.
  • A group moment. One big singalong or line dance pulls in the people who swore they weren't dancing.

Keep it right for the room

Clean versions, all night. That's non-negotiable at a work event. Beyond that, ask your DJ to keep a do-not-play list from your team, and to keep the volume at a level where people can still talk at the bar. More on lyrics in clean vs. explicit music. It's written for weddings, but the same rules apply.

Logistics planners forget

  • Certificate of insurance. Many venues and office buildings require one from every vendor. Ask early.
  • Load-in. Freight elevators, loading docks and building hours all affect setup time.
  • Power. An outlet near the DJ that isn't shared with catering equipment.
  • The end time. Know when the lights come up and when vendors need to be out.
  • One point of contact. Someone on your team who can make calls on the night.
  • Book early. December Fridays and Saturdays are the first dates to go.

My take

Keep the program short, open the floor the second it ends and keep the music clean. The rest is reading the room.

Quick answers

Should we play holiday music all night?

A little goes a long way. A few holiday classics during arrival set the mood. After that, mix in songs everyone knows. Not everyone celebrates the same holidays, and a full night of holiday music wears thin.

How long should speeches and awards be?

Shorter than you think. Keep the program tight, with a clear run of show and walk-up music, so the energy is still there when the dance floor opens.

Will the music stay clean?

Yes. Corporate events get clean versions all night, and I keep it right for the room.

Can you set up at our office?

Yes. I size the sound to the space and work with your building's rules for load-in, elevators and noise.

When should we book a DJ for a December party?

As soon as you have a date. December Fridays and Saturdays are the first to go.
