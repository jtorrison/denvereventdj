Outdoor Ceremony Sound Checklist

Outdoor ceremonies are some of my favorites, and they're where sound goes wrong most often. No walls, no outlet, wind in the mics and a long walk from the parking lot. Here's the checklist I work through so every word of your vows gets heard.

By Josh Torrison, Denver wedding and event DJ since 2011

In this guide

  1. When you pick the spot
  2. A month out
  3. The week of
  4. On the day
  5. What it looks like for real

You don't need to do any of this yourself. A good DJ will. But knowing the list helps you ask the right questions, and spot the gaps before the day.

When you pick the spot

  • Find the nearest outlet. If there isn't one, that's fine. Just know now, so your DJ plans for battery power.
  • Stand where the guests will sit. Is there a road, a river, a generator or a kitchen fan nearby? Those all compete with your vows.
  • Check the sun at ceremony time. Guests squinting into the sun don't hear well either, and gear in full afternoon sun gets hot.
  • Ask about sound rules. Some sites have quiet hours or no-amplified-sound rules. More in questions to ask your venue.

A month out

  • Confirm the mic plan. A clip-on mic for the officiant, a handheld for vows and readings. If anyone is singing or playing live, tell your DJ now.
  • Pick your songs and check their length. Guest arrival, the wedding party, your entrance and the recessional. The processional song calculator tells you if a song is too short, and a custom edit fixes it.
  • Decide who cues the music. Someone has to watch for the moment the wedding party lines up. That's the DJ's job, not a cousin's.
  • Know the weather backup and whether it has power.
  • Check the walk. How far is the ceremony spot from parking and from the reception? Gear has to get there, and maybe move again after.

The week of

  • Send the final order of events: who walks, in what order, and who reads.
  • Download every song. Mountain venues and big open sites often have no cell service. I download everything before I leave home, so nothing depends on a signal.
  • Watch the forecast and agree on when the rain or wind call gets made.
  • Confirm setup time with the venue so sound is tested before guests arrive.

On the day

  • Speakers aimed at the seats, not over them, so the back rows hear as clearly as the front.
  • Mics placed for the wind. A clip-on tucked out of the wind beats a handheld in a gust, and windscreens help.
  • Cables taped down or kept to the edges, never across the aisle.
  • A quick sound check with the officiant before guests sit down.
  • Arrival music on as the first guests walk up.
  • A plan for after. If cocktail hour is somewhere else, it needs its own speaker or time to move gear. The speaker setup checker maps it out.

What it looks like for real

At Boulder Flower Farm, the ceremony spot under the trees had no outlet. I ran two powered speakers and both mics on battery power, so there were no cords across the lawn and the back rows heard every word.

At Crestview Ranch, a windstorm rolled in at noon and we found out the ceremony site had no power. I drove into town for two 100-foot extension cords and ran power out to the site. It worked, and it's exactly why "where's the nearest outlet?" belongs at the top of this list.

My take

Ask about power the day you pick the spot. Everything else on this list is easy to fix. Power on the wedding day isn't.

Quick answers

Do we need a microphone for a small outdoor ceremony?

Usually, yes. Outdoors there are no walls to carry voices, and wind and traffic eat them fast. Even 40 guests will hear the vows better with a clip-on mic on the officiant and a small speaker.

What if there's no outlet at the ceremony spot?

Battery-powered speakers and wireless mics. They run the whole ceremony with no cords across the lawn. If your DJ doesn't have them, ask early, so it isn't solved with a generator or a long cord on the day.

How early should sound be set up for an outdoor ceremony?

Before the first guests arrive, with time for a quick sound check. Guest arrival music starts as people walk up, so plan setup around that, not the ceremony start time.

What happens to the sound plan if it rains?

It moves with you. Know the backup space ahead of time, make sure it has power, and plan when the call gets made so the sound can be set up there before guests arrive.
