Questions to Ask Your Wedding Venue About Music and Sound

Most music surprises on a wedding day trace back to a question nobody asked the venue: a hard curfew, no power at the ceremony spot, a long walk from parking. Here's the list I'd ask, and why each one matters.

By Josh Torrison, Denver wedding and event DJ since 2011

In this guide

  1. Sound limits and curfews
  2. Power
  3. Spaces and the room flip
  4. Load-in and parking
  5. House sound and AV rules
  6. Weather backup
  7. Insurance and paperwork
  8. The short checklist

You don't need to become a sound expert. You just need the answers, and then you hand them to your DJ. A good DJ will ask most of these anyway, but couples who ask early avoid the expensive surprises, like finding out a month out that the ceremony spot has no power.

Sound limits and curfews

  • Is there a music curfew? Is it a hard stop or a "turn it down" time?
  • Is there a volume (decibel) limit, and does the venue measure it?
  • Does the music have to move indoors after a certain time?

Why it matters: the whole evening gets planned backward from the end time. At Boulder Flower Farm, a hard 10 PM stop shaped the timeline, and the last song landed right on time because we planned for it. Mountain towns and backyard venues often have firm quiet hours too. More in mountain weddings in Colorado.

Power

  • Where are the outlets near the ceremony spot, cocktail area and dance floor?
  • Are they on separate circuits from the kitchen and catering?
  • Is there power in the tent, or does it need a generator?

Why it matters: the best ceremony spot is rarely next to an outlet. If there's no power, your DJ needs to know so they bring battery-powered speakers. At Crestview Ranch, we found out on the wedding day that the ceremony site had no power. I drove to town for extension cords and made it work, but it's an easy thing to ask in advance.

Spaces and the room flip

  • Where do the ceremony, cocktail hour, dinner and dancing each happen?
  • If the ceremony and reception share a space, how long does the flip take, and where do guests go?
  • How far apart are the spaces?

Why it matters: every separate space needs its own sound, or time to move gear. The speaker setup checker turns your answers into a plan.

Load-in and parking

  • When can vendors load in, and when do they have to be out?
  • Where do vendors park, and how far is the walk to each space? Any stairs or elevators?
  • Is there a day-of contact for vendors?

Why it matters: sound needs to be set up and tested before guests arrive. A long walk or a tight load-in window changes when your DJ shows up.

House sound and AV rules

  • Does the venue have its own sound system or mics? What exactly?
  • Is there a required or preferred AV company?
  • Any rules on haze, lasers or extra lighting?

Why it matters: a house system might cover speeches and background music, but rarely a full dance floor. And some venues don't allow haze, because it can set off smoke detectors.

Weather backup

  • What's the rain or wind plan, and when does the venue make the call?
  • Is there power in the backup space?

Why it matters: Colorado afternoons turn fast. At Crestview Ranch, a windstorm moved cocktail hour and dinner indoors four hours before the ceremony. The music plan has to work in the backup space too. More in outdoor and backyard weddings.

Insurance and paperwork

  • Does the venue require a certificate of insurance (COI) from vendors? By when?
  • Any vendor contract or registration with the venue?

The short checklist

Copy this into an email to your venue coordinator:

  • Music curfew and any volume limit
  • Outlets near the ceremony spot, cocktail area and dance floor
  • Where each part of the day happens, and the flip time between them
  • Vendor load-in time, parking and the walk
  • House sound system, preferred AV and lighting rules
  • Rain and wind backup, and whether it has power
  • COI requirements for vendors

Quick answers

When should we ask the venue these questions?

Before you sign with the venue if you can, and again a month or two out. Sound limits and power can rule out a dream plan, so it's better to know early.

What if the venue has a sound limit?

It's very workable. Aiming the speakers in toward the dance floor and using a subwoofer carefully keeps the party feeling full at a lower volume. Get the limit and the curfew in writing and share them with your DJ.

Does our DJ need insurance?

Many venues require a certificate of insurance (COI) from every vendor. Ask the venue what it requires and have your DJ send it ahead of time. I carry liability insurance and send COIs on request.

Can our DJ just use the venue's sound system?

Sometimes, for speeches and background music. House systems are rarely built for a dance floor, so ask what the venue has and let your DJ decide what to bring.
