Do You Need an MC at Your Wedding?

Yes, someone needs to be on the mic. That person welcomes your entrance, calls guests to dinner, gathers everyone for toasts and the first dance, and keeps the timeline moving. It can be your DJ or a friend. What matters is that it's someone who knows the plan.

By Josh Torrison, Denver wedding and event DJ since 2011

What a wedding MC actually does

Think of the MC as the person who connects the moments. Most of the job happens in short announcements:

  • Letting guests know the ceremony is about to start
  • The grand entrance, including introducing the wedding party
  • Calling guests to dinner and releasing tables for the buffet
  • Gathering everyone for toasts, the first dance and parent dances
  • Cake cutting, bouquet toss or any traditions you're including
  • Last call and the send-off

Behind the scenes, the MC is checking in with your planner, the venue and the photographer so nobody misses a moment. If the photographer is changing a battery when you're about to cut the cake, a good MC waits ten seconds.

Your DJ or a friend?

Both can work. A friend or your officiant on the mic can feel personal and warm. Just know they'll spend part of your wedding working, and they need to know the timeline cold.

When your DJ handles it, the music and the announcements line up automatically. The song fades as the announcement starts, and the next song hits as it ends. I'm happy to do as much or as little as you want. Some couples want me to run everything. Some want a friend to do the entrance and me to handle the rest.

How much talking is too much

My style is light. Short, clear announcements, then back to the music. I'm not going to run games or hype the crowd unless you want that energy. Some couples do, and that's great too. Tell me how you picture it and I'll match it.

Keeping toasts on track

  • Set the order ahead of time and share it with your MC.
  • Give speakers a rough length. Two to three minutes each is plenty.
  • Plan the mic handoff. A wireless handheld that gets passed hand to hand works best. I'll show each speaker how to hold it so everyone in the back can hear.
  • Time it with dinner. Many couples do toasts right as dinner is served, so nobody is waiting on food.

When plans change

Weddings rarely run exactly on time, and that's fine. At one wedding, everyone was seated when a family emergency delayed the ceremony. I kept the music going and kept guests in the loop ("about five more minutes, folks") until everyone was ready. That's the real job of an MC: making sure nobody is standing around wondering what's next.

My take

The best MC is the one you barely notice. The night just flows.
