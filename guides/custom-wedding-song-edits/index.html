Custom Wedding Song Edits: What's Possible

Your first dance is four and a half minutes. Your walk-down song ends before you reach the arbor. Your parent dance song has one line you'd rather Grandma not hear. A custom edit fixes all of that, and it can turn a big moment into the start of the party.

By Josh Torrison, Denver wedding and event DJ since 2011

In this guide

  1. What a custom edit is
  2. What an edit can do
  3. Real edits from real weddings
  4. How it works

Custom edits are one of my favorite parts of planning a wedding. They're a small detail that makes a moment feel like it was written for you.

What a custom edit is

An edit is a new version of a song, cut and rearranged so it fits your moment. Same song, same artist, same feel. It's just the right length, it goes where you want it to, and it skips anything you don't want playing in front of your family.

What an edit can do

  • Make a song longer. When the walk-down song ends halfway down the aisle, I loop the best section so it lasts the whole walk. The processional song calculator tells you how long you need.
  • Make a song shorter. Three minutes feels long when everyone is watching. A first dance can end on a high note at two and a half.
  • Blend two songs. Two songs that both mean something, one moment. Common for parent dances and entrances.
  • Remove a lyric. Drop the one line you'd rather skip, without the radio edit that changes everything else. More on lyrics in clean vs. explicit wedding music.
  • Turn a moment into a party. A first dance that starts slow and intimate, then drops into a full dance mix so the floor opens up the second it ends.

How it works

  1. Send me the song and what you want: longer, shorter, a blend, a lyric gone, a drop into the party.
  2. We talk it through. A quick call about the moment, the timing and the parts of the song that matter most to you.
  3. I make the edit and send it over.
  4. You listen and tell me what to change.
  5. It plays on the day, cued at exactly the right moment. If I'm not your DJ, you get the file for your DJ or your playlist.

Planning every song at once? The wedding music planner has a box to flag the moments you want edited.

Tips for a great edit

  • Know your timing. Time your walk, or measure the aisle and use the calculator. For a first dance, decide how long you actually want to be out there.
  • Pick the parts that matter. The chorus you love, the line from your first date, the build at the end.
  • Don't over-edit. The best edits sound like the original song. Nobody should notice.
  • Listen where you'll hear it. Play it through real speakers, or in the car, not just on your phone.
  • Ask early. A few weeks out leaves time for a round of changes.

My take

Keep the song you love. Let the edit make it fit.

Quick answers

Can you make an edit if you're not our DJ?

Yes. Send me the song and what you want it to do, and I'll make the edit and send you the file for your DJ or your playlist.

Will anyone hear where the song was cut?

That's the goal. A good edit follows the song's beat and phrasing, so it sounds like the song was always that way.

Can you take out one bad word without using the radio edit?

Usually, yes. Many radio edits change more than they need to. A custom edit can drop just the lyric you don't want, and keep the rest of the song the way you love it.

How far ahead should we ask?

A few weeks before the wedding is ideal. That leaves time to listen, ask for a change and listen again.
