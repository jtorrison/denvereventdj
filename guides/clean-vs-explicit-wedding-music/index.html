Clean or Explicit? Handling Lyrics at Your Wedding

Most couples go clean early and loosen up later. Play clean versions while kids and grandparents are on the floor, then decide whether explicit versions are okay once the late-night crowd takes over. Tell your DJ your rule ahead of time so you never have to think about it that night.

By Josh Torrison, Denver wedding and event DJ since 2011

In this guide

  2. Your three options

Start with your crowd

Lyrics are really a question about who's in the room. A wedding with lots of kids, grandparents or a conservative side of the family needs a different rule than a late-night party with your college friends. Most weddings have both crowds, just at different times of the night.

Your three options

1. Clean all night

The safe choice for family-heavy weddings, religious venues, and any event with kids on the dance floor. Nearly every popular song has a radio edit, so you lose very little.

2. Clean early, explicit later

The most common choice. Clean through dinner, the big moments and the first stretch of dancing. Once the kids head home and the late-night crowd takes over, explicit versions are fair game. You can pick a time, or let your DJ read the room.

3. Anything goes

Works for an adults-only wedding where everyone knows every word. Even then, keep the ceremony, dinner and family dances clean. Nobody wants a surprise lyric during the father-daughter dance.

When the clean version ruins the song

Some radio edits are so chopped up the song stops making sense. If one of your must-plays is like that, there are options. I can play it later in the night when your explicit rule kicks in, find a better clean edit, or make a custom edit that removes one line you don't want playing in front of your family.

Tell your DJ up front

  • Decide your rule before the wedding: clean all night, a switch time, or anything goes.
  • Flag any specific songs that are off limits, clean version or not.
  • Decide how requests work. A guest asking for an explicit song at 7:30 is a lot easier to handle when the answer is already decided.
  • Put it all in your do-not-play list, which I treat as a hard rule.

For corporate events and school dances, it's simple: clean, all night. See corporate events and parties and campus events.

My take

Nobody remembers that a song was the clean version. They do remember a lyric that made grandma leave the dance floor.
